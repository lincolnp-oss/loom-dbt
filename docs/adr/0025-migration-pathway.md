# ADR 0025: The adoption pathway is assessment-first and proof-gated

Status: Accepted

## Context

The ask is a pathway that brings an *existing* dbt project onto Loom: the opposite direction from everything else Loom does. The input is arbitrary - any project's models, macros and habits - while Loom's generators cover eighteen narrow structural shapes and its fit rules deliberately cover only three of them (staging, bronze, silver). Most real models will not fit a generator, and a wrong "fits" is far worse than a missed one, because accepting a proposal replaces a working model.

The pathway's first shape had three separate output tiers - registration drafts, conversion draft sets and authoring scaffolds - and treated a registration as pure inventory knowledge that said nothing about conversion intent. The owner's manual validation of that shape found the costs: three artefact classes made promotion a translation step between formats, and models a practitioner had deliberately registered were still drafted for conversion. The pathway below is the consolidated shape that replaced it; the tier design and the knowledge-only registration stance were retired for those findings, not for taste.

## Decision

**One assessment, two commands.** `loom register` reports every project-owned model's inventory state and drafts a registration for each unknown model; `loom convert` drafts generator conversions; `loom plan` (ADR 0011) stands alone. Each command emits its half of one report (JSON with `--format json`), and draft files are written only on an explicit write run (`--write`, under the CLI's one write convention). For every project-owned model the report records its state - `controlled_by_loom` (a generated instance), `known_not_controlled` (a registration) or `unknown_to_loom` (manifest only) - a *drafted registration* for unknown models (classification suggestion with its evidence), a *generator-fit proposal* where a conservative rule matches (evidence and drafted parameters recorded), or the specific reason no fit is proposed. Interface commitments a swap would put at risk are flagged on the model: an enforced dbt `contract: {enforced: true}` (ADR 0032), Mesh access that differs from the dbt default, attached tests, and downstream consumers decomposed by kind. The report also accounts for what it does not assess: non-model resources are counted by type in a seen-but-not-assessed summary, the manifest's semantic-layer sections among them, and each model's record names the metrics and saved queries reachable from it through its semantic models - reporting only; generating semantic-layer objects is outside Loom's boundary (ADR 0007). Snapshots and other stateful resources are never proposed: they cannot be equivalence-proven by the standard below.

**Registration is the conversion gate.** Convert drafts only models unknown to Loom by default. Registering a model withdraws it from the drafting pool; deleting the registration returns it; `--include-registered` restores the full drafting reach, and registered models are still counted and reported either way. The playbook order follows: convert first, register the rest.

**One unified draft, authored in final form.** A draft is a single artefact per assessed model under `local/drafts/` - a review area Loom never loads as inventory: a draft record (the generator and its basis, the evidence or no-fit account, the declared interface, review TODOs, the exact promotion steps), a final-form instance document in the shape `metadata/instances/` consumes, and, when the project has no pattern for the generator, one synthesised pattern definition per generator under a final name the instances reference from the start. The declared interface - the model's tests (built-in and project-defined generic tests copied verbatim, ADR 0029), unit tests, semantic models and config facts - is standard furniture of every draft, not of one tier. Promotion is **move-only**: complete the TODOs, move the files into `metadata/` unchanged, delete any registration the instance replaces, and gate on `loom validate`. There are no content edits and no rename step at promotion time, and promotion itself is unpoliced - the validator's rejection of unfilled `__TODO__` sentinels is the hard backstop.

**Holes are named; readiness is derived, not stored.** A drafted mapping keeps exact matches and one guarded rename inference; every unpaired documented output is drafted as an unresolved placeholder (`source: __TODO__`), with the evidence naming the unresolved outputs and the candidate source columns. Constructs no generator in scope can express - a row filter, a plain-SQL column expression, a `LIMIT` - are named readiness blockers, never silently dropped. Each model's readiness account is re-derived on every run from the draft's current content plus current evidence, and lives in the draft record and the JSON report, never inside the instance file: *ready to promote* (zero holes, zero blockers), *needs authoring* (with its hole count, each hole kinded authoring - only the practitioner can supply it - or evidence - Loom could not verify it, closable from either side), or *not draftable*. The report's headline is the distribution across those three states, and a change against the on-disk draft's previous account is reported as a transition.

**Fit rules are conservative and evidence-recording.** The rules cover the shapes with crisp signatures - staging (one source dependency, no model dependencies, no aggregation/join/window signals), bronze (one source dependency, a plain pass-through of its source), and silver (one model dependency plus a DISTINCT or ROW_NUMBER dedup shape with an extractable key) - and abstain, with the construct named, on shapes they cannot certify: a dedup QUALIFY predicate with an OR escape, dotted struct-field extraction, row filters, plain-SQL expressions, `LIMIT`. Everything else is a recorded no-fit with its blocking signals, not a guess. Shape analysis is stdlib-only: sqlglot remains a development dependency, and adding a runtime dependency is a separate product decision.

**Equivalence before swap.** A fit proposal is never applied directly. The adoption playbook's warehouse proof generates the candidate, builds the original and the candidate against identical source data in scratch projects, and compares contents - row counts plus a checksum over every column; only a pass licenses the swap, and the generated-file ownership rules keep it explicit and reversible (the hand-written file is removed by the practitioner, never silently overwritten). A static pre-proof stage, `loom prove`, runs before the warehouse act over two practitioner-compiled manifests at fixed names in `local/equivalence/` and returns exactly two verdicts per model - *proven*, where the warehouse comparison may be skipped, or *inconclusive*, naming why, where the warehouse proof decides exactly as before. There is no static disproof. Declared differences are handled by one rule only: a candidate-only `__`-prefixed column is a declared difference; there are no declared mappings, and an original column without a candidate counterpart makes the comparison inconclusive. Stateful models are never statically proven, and Loom never shells to dbt to produce the compile manifests.

**Local-only, on a guarded manifest.** The pathway runs entirely locally: the manifest, the inventory and the project files are read in place, and nothing is sent to a hosted service. The assessment shares its manifest freshness and staleness checks with coverage and source inspection - the manifest's project identity and timestamp are verified against the project's current files before it is read, and a stale or wrong-project manifest is refused outright, so no adoption decision is ever taken from evidence about a different or outdated project state. Both commands hard-fail on a stale manifest, naming the stale files, before producing any output. Fit calibration assumes a parse-produced manifest; a build-produced manifest is annotated at the point of use - the report declares its compilation provenance and each fit carries its basis - never silently trusted and never gated on it.

## Consequences

Any dbt project can reach full Loom inventory in one reviewed pass (assessment → drafts → `loom validate`), which is valuable even when no model is ever converted. Conversions proceed model by model under an empirical proof standard rather than trust in the classifier, and the readiness distribution is the project's honest adoption account: what can move now, what needs authoring, and what Loom cannot draft at all. The primary deliverable is the playbook plus this report; whether a dedicated end-to-end adoption command is warranted is an owner judgement to make after the playbook has been used.
